# Pilot: AS1.5 Make Ten

**Objective:** use the make-ten strategy to add across 10, e.g. 8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13.
**Stage 1 · Year 1 · borrowed from Japan (Grade 1 *sakuranbo keisan*, "cherry calculation").**
**Playable prototype:** `make-ten.html`. Open it in a browser on a tablet. Progress is saved on that device.

This document follows one child through the objective from the day before he starts to long after he has mastered it. It shows at each step what he sees, what he does, and what the app decides.

---

## Why this objective

Most 6-year-olds who struggle answer 8 + 5 by counting on with their fingers: "9, 10, 11, 12, 13". That works but is slow and error-prone, and it hits a wall with 38 + 25. Make-ten replaces counting with **structure**: fill a ten, then see what is left. Every later addition and subtraction method, including column addition, is built on this idea.

## Step 0: Is he ready? (prerequisites)

Make-ten needs two earlier skills. The app checks both before the lesson unlocks.

| Prerequisite | What he must show | If he can't |
|---|---|---|
| **AS1.1 Bonds to 10** | Sees 8 counters on a ten-frame and says "2 more" within about 3 seconds | Do the bonds-to-10 lessons first (fill-the-frame games) |
| **NS1.4 Teen numbers as 10 and some more** | Sees a full ten and 3 more and says "13" without counting the ten | Do the teen-number lessons first (build 13 as a full frame and 3) |

## Day 1: First lesson (about 12 minutes)

### 1. Watch (about 1 minute)
He does nothing but watch. **No rules are spoken before he sees them happen.**

1. 8 red counters drop into the left ten-frame one by one; the tag **8** appears above.
2. 5 yellow counters drop into the right frame; tag **5**.
3. The 2 empty spaces in the red frame **glow**: "8 needs 2 more to make ten."
4. Under the 5, a **cherry** grows: 5 splits into **2** and **3**. The 2 lights up.
5. **Two yellow counters slide across** and fill the glowing spaces.
6. A dashed ring wraps the full frame; the tags change to **10** and **3**.
7. The equation appears last, as a description of what he has just seen: **8 + 5 = 10 + 3 = 13**.

He can tap **Watch again**, or **Watch with 9 + 4** to see the idea with different numbers. He moves on when he presses **My turn**.

### 2. Try (about 2 minutes): he does it with his own fingers
- **9 + 4**: he drags yellow counters into the red frame. Each counter snaps into the next empty space and the tags update live.
- He **cannot over-fill**. A counter dropped on a full frame bounces back, so the ten-frame itself teaches when to stop.
- **Stuck for 7 seconds?** A faint ghost counter glides from the yellow frame to the next empty space. No words.
- When the frame is full, the cherry fills itself in (4 splits into **1** and **3**) to show the split he has just made.
- "How many altogether?" He taps 13, 12 or 14. A wrong tap gets a visual explanation ("look, there are 3 outside the ten") and he tries again.
- Then **8 + 6**.

### 3. Practise (about 4 minutes): the picture fades
| Item | What he sees | What he does |
|---|---|---|
| 7 + 5 | Solid counters | Drags, as in Try |
| 8 + 7 | **Outline dots** (sketch) | Taps "how many to make ten?" (2), then "5 is 2 and ?" (3); the dots then slide across to confirm |
| 9 + 6 | Outline dots | Same |
| 7 + 6 | **Numbers and an empty cherry only** | Fills the cherry and the answer. **Show me** brings the frames back for a moment |

The picture fades one step at a time, from objects to a sketch to symbols. The model is always one tap away.

### 4. Check (about 3 minutes)
- 10 random questions (6 + 5 up to 9 + 9), numbers only, answered on a keypad.
- **Show me** is available but **recorded**.
- A wrong answer is never just marked wrong: the full animation replays quickly with those numbers, so he sees why.
- The app records the score, whether Show me was used, and his **typical answer time**.

### 5. Teach Dot (about 2 minutes): only if the check was passed
Dot (the mascot) makes a mistake: "8 + 5, I split the 5 into **3** and 2." The animation plays Dot's split: the ten overflows. He picks the right split, and the correct animation plays. Spotting and fixing a mistake is a stronger sign of understanding than getting the right answer, and it replaces "explain it in words", which is hard at 6.

## Day 2 and later: short sessions (about 6 minutes)

Each later session skips the full lesson:
1. **Warm-up**: one sketch-mode item (fading kept fresh).
2. **Practise**: one symbols-only cherry item.
3. **Check**: 10 questions, as above.
4. **Teach Dot** if passed.

If he fails the check, the next session replays **Watch** and **Try** before the check.

## When is it mastered?

All three conditions must hold:

1. **Accurate:** at least 9/10 on the check,
2. **on 3 different days** (one good day can be luck; three days show he has kept it),
3. and the **final passing check without Show me** (he no longer needs the picture on screen).

The parent panel also shows **typical answer time**. If he is accurate but slow (over about 6 seconds), he is probably still counting in ones in his head. In that case the app gives extra sketch-mode practice before calling it mastered. In the full app this becomes an automatic rule; in the pilot, look at the number yourself.

## After mastery: excellence and review

### Excellence challenges (unlock on mastery)
Going beyond the National Curriculum minimum:
1. **Missing number:** 8 + ? = 13 (make-ten in reverse).
2. **Two ways:** 7 + 8 can be done by giving 3 to the 7 or 2 to the 8. Which is quicker?
3. **Bigger numbers:** 28 + 5 = 30 + 3 = 33. The same idea with tens: this is where it starts to pay off.

### Spaced review (full app)
The skill comes back as a warm-up item after **7, 21 and 60 days**. A miss drops it back to *practising* and replays the Watch animation once.

### What it unlocks
- **AS1.6 Subtract back through 10** (13 - 5 = 13 - 3 - 2), the mirror image of this method.
- **AS2.2 Two-digit addition with exchange** (46 + 27), where the ten-frame becomes a ten rod.

## What to watch for when you try it with him

| You see | It means | Do |
|---|---|---|
| He watches the demo twice before trying | Good: he is building the picture | Nothing |
| He moves counters but counts every red one first | Not yet subitising 8 on a frame | Practise NS1.7 ten-frame flashes |
| Fine with counters, stuck when they turn into outline dots | Relying on touch, not yet picturing it | Stay on sketch items longer and ask "close your eyes, where do the 2 go?" |
| Right answers but slow (over 6 s) | Still counting in his head | More practise rounds before moving on |
| Explains "I gave 2 to make ten" | Understanding | Celebrate and move on |

Off the screen, put 8 sweets in an egg box with 10 holes, add 5 more, and ask him how many go in the box and how many are left over. Real objects plus the app is the strongest combination.

## Pilot limitations

- Voice uses the browser's built-in English voice; the real app will use recorded prompts.
- Progress is stored only on the device. Use **"Pilot: pretend it's the next day"** in the parent panel to see the 3-day mastery path quickly.
- Spaced review and automatic routing to prerequisite lessons are described here but not built in the pilot.
